# Level design and rapid visual authoring

Lantern uses stable, authored encounter areas with constrained procedural decoration. The same area builder, assets, lighting and renderer serve gameplay and the local authoring preview. No public deployment is part of this workflow.

## Area standard

The saved standard stage spans **2 viewport widths × 2 viewport heights** of ground coverage at its original 1920 × 1080 calibration. This is four screens of ground, not two screens total. That calibration used zoom 1.45 and a 45-degree downward pitch: **33.103 × 26.334 m**, measured along the camera's ground-plane right/forward axes. One screen is 16.552 × 13.167 m. The envelope is rotated 45 degrees relative to world X/Z.

`npm run levels:calibrate` intersects the current camera corner rays with Y=0 and prints an envelope for new definitions. Today’s camera uses a 35-degree downward pitch and Default Camera Distance multiplier 0.6 (zoom 1/0.6), so its output differs from the saved standard above. Copy the result when deliberately creating a new size standard. Dimensions are saved in scene data; browser resizing, Camera Distance choices and future camera edits never resize existing stages. Forest Clearing uses this standard envelope, a woodland approach, one guarded camp and a separate caster glade; the old circular clearing has been replaced.

Use metres, Y-up, yaw in radians, positive explicit scale and stable IDs. Flat ground defaults to Y=0; authored beds and traversal ground may extend below zero. A 4 m decorative apron surrounds the design envelope. It is scenery, not additional traversable space. A flat terrain backdrop may extend beyond that apron to cover the allowed camera views; navigation and prop placement remain bounded by the area and decorative apron. Keep ordinary areas mostly flat; explicit movement trials may use shared rendered/collision surface triangles for small slopes and steps. Use banks, cliffs and ruins around the playable space for larger elevation. Terrain asset exports happen separately from placement iterations.

Each area needs a landmark, readable combat space, a clear approach and exit, and a decorative perimeter. Compose from one representative gameplay-camera view at normal scale. Other positions, zooms and viewport sizes are targeted tools when a visible concern warrants them. Avoid foreground objects concealing actors, weapon contact or gate openings. Small areas should look deliberate, not uniformly filled.

## Definitions and ownership

Definitions live in `src/levels/areas/` as version-1 JSON, typed by `src/levels/types.ts`. The registry discovers them. `src/levels/builder.ts` owns rendered area instances; simulation consumes only numeric spawns/boundaries. The builder retains shared GLB/catalog resources across area replacement and releases area-owned meshes, instancing buffers, lights and effects.

- **Envelope:** fixed world dimensions, yaw, apron and reference projection.
- **Layout:** player spawn, authored enemy list or legacy enemy/optional caster spawns, and circle or counterclockwise convex-polygon boundary.
- **Props:** stable ID, asset or primitive, transform, shadow policy and foliage role. Mark ground surfaces `terrain:true`; other new-area scenery must stay outside reserved regions. Primitive sizes are `[width,height,depth]` for boxes, `[topRadius,bottomRadius,height,segments]` for cylinders, `[radius]` for faceted rocks and `[width,height,depth]` for a simple canvas A-frame tent (double-sided). Optional `height` retains legacy model-height normalization. Prefer explicit scale for new assets. Optional height normalization centers the asset and places its bottom at the authored position before placement yaw/scale; rotation must not shift an off-center asset away from its collision proxy.
- **Lighting:** inherit the sole shared Golden preset through `"lighting": {}` or author narrow `overrides` for fog distance, sun coverage and probe volume/settings. Full legacy lighting definitions and profile selection are unsupported. Palette, strength, environment and grading belong to the shared preset. Static scenery changes invalidate probe appearances; gameplay-only changes retain them; cancelled changes retain the previous lighting. See [lighting authoring](LIGHTING.md) for supported fields and preparation.
- **Effects:** water and local braziers; static composition belongs to area data.
- **Reserved regions:** circular combat, arrival and route exclusions. Build complex clear routes from overlapping regions.
- **Scatter:** seeded primitive decoration; bounded attempts can yield fewer than the requested count. `excludedIds` removes generated objects. An explicit prop with a generated ID replaces it. Decoration stays inside the walk boundary and outside reserved regions, gate triggers and automatic 2 m arrival clearances.
- **Grass clumps:** optional `grass` regions use stable `id`, `center: [x,z]`, `radii: [x,z]` (positive metres, at most 20), `yaw` (radians), `clumpsPerM2` (positive, at most 4) and optional `excludedIds`. `grassVariants` supplies `{ asset: { libraryId }, weight, height: [min,max] }`; heights are metres before placement scale. `grassLimit` caps manual and generated clumps together, at most 1,000 per woodland area and 200 in Graveyard Ruins. Crypt remains bare. Overlapping ellipses merge density rather than doubling it; feathered edges and seeded growth break up distribution. Fixed half-metre candidate cells produce stable `grass-p3-n8` IDs (signed X/Z cell coordinates), transforms and broad botanical groups. Removing a clump does not move neighbours. Put its ID in any region's `excludedIds` to remove it, or add an ordinary asset prop with the same ID to replace it. Other grass props referencing the area's palette add manual clumps; they use the same batches and count toward the cap. Overrides take precedence over exclusions. Manual placements retain position, yaw, scale and optional height, with bottom-centred source normalization; avoid negative/zero scale and gameplay/lighting-only metadata. Grass always receives shadows, casts none and has no outlines. Roots and conservative scaled footprints exclude reserved/gate/arrival space, collision boxes, strongly worn soil, paths and water. Invalid manual footprints report incomplete art. Cells share borrowed source geometry/materials and use the shared vegetation wind, character parting, paused clock and FSR previous-frame deformation. Grass is cosmetic, never changes navigation, and stays outside static irradiance capture. Terrain coverage and wet-bank masks retain their existing texture binding.
- **Views:** entrance, center, exit, review-1 and review-2 ground targets. Overview is computed separately.
- **Gathering nodes:** props and scatter may author `harvest: { kind: "tree" | "stone" | "iron", radius?, level?, baseYield?, contacts? }`. `src/levels/resources.ts` combines this metadata with identified standing trees. Defaults use resource level 1, yield 1 and three contacts; keep mineral collision proxies explicit. Stable placement IDs preserve depletion through travel, independently of optional art. [Gathering](GATHERING.md) owns progression, collected-drop XP and renewal.
- **Shelter:** optional `shelter` supplies `position: [x,z]`, `yaw` and `stash: [x,z]` for the damaged/repaired cover and its chest. Keep approaches and collision proxies aligned with both appearances. The shared recipe belongs to `src/gameplay/skills.ts`; permanent restoration, stash contents and Rested belong to Adventure.

Ordinary props do not automatically become colliders or navigation obstacles. Harvestable trees are the exception: `src/levels/trees.ts` identifies standing-tree assets and explicit harvest metadata, reuses a matching proxy or derives a trunk proxy, and removes/restores it during felling/regrowth. Optional `traversal.obstacles` defines explicit box proxies with world position, full size and yaw. Optional `traversal.surfaces` defines shared rendered/collision triangles for slopes. Keep proxies aligned with their art and spawns/routes clear; optional art does not change gameplay geometry. The numeric boundary generates the base floor. Optional `effects.portals` defines generic cosmetic portals (position, yaw, width, height), not gate travel. See [library trials](LIBRARY_TRIALS.md) for the movement adapter and comparison URLs.

Repeated compatible static primitives and opaque asset meshes are instanced. Skins, wind, transparency and native LOD ownership remain separate; catalog LODs are updated from the gameplay camera. Tiny decoration does not cast shadows by default. Use one main sun shadow light; Homestead and Clearing also use shadowed campfire point lights. The lighting pass currently prioritizes quality; shadowed point lights require six captures. Sun coverage follows the visible ground and raised scenery with texel-snapped positioning. Share materials and keep accent lights, foliage overlap and full-screen effects restrained. Baked static detail is an optional asset-preparation task, not a requirement for every iteration.

New scene references and appearance substitutions can remain Unreviewed through the [Asset Review Lab](ASSET_REVIEW.md). Denied or deletion-marked uses block shipping; the change-aware gate compares new uses against the integration base. Review metadata does not automatically edit scene placements or build selection.

Use `npm run levels:find -- --query pine` for a compact private catalog lookup with IDs, bounds and conversion warnings. Standalone assets use `/vendor/...` URLs. Wider library assets use catalog IDs. Run `npm run levels:assets` explicitly to add referenced catalog IDs to build selection; it retains existing selections. `npm run levels:check` reports missing selections and catalog entries. Optional absent art is reported; structural validation remains usable in asset-free CI.

## Entrances, exits and travel

Encounter areas have one primary entrance, one primary exit and optional branch gates. A destination area may set `terminal: true` to omit the primary exit and return through its entrance. Safe areas (`kind: "safe"`) may omit gates and the enemy spawn. Gate positions are authored; there are no mandatory compass sides. Use a 3 m opening and 2 m-deep trigger by default. Yaw defines the trigger's forward axis: `[sin(yaw), cos(yaw)]` in X/Z. Place the destination arrival at least 2 m inside its walkable boundary and face inward. Reserve the arrival and approach for movement; exclude small decoration and keep large scenery outside the opening.

Links name a destination area and gate. New entries use that gate's arrival transform while retaining character health and session enemy/drop state. A short fade pauses input while content loads. Arrival triggers cannot immediately send the player back before they leave the trigger. One area is active at a time; shared assets remain cached. Victory permits walking to an exit; defeat offers Return Home with restored health. Inactive areas retain enemy health/defeat and ground drops through application relaunch. Individual 45-minute active-play deadlines continue in other areas; pending renewal is applied safely on entry or out of view during play.

Normal startup begins at Homestead for a new character or resumes at the last safe campfire. Forest Clearing’s authored player spawn is halfway between the camp and Homestead path; explicit development authoring/render-inspection routes can select that area/spawn; ordinary area URLs still open Title. Homestead is a safe area: a compact dusk woodland camp inside the standard envelope, with natural scenery that can later be cleared for building and an optional repairable shelter. Healing campfires restore 3% of maximum health per gameplay second within 3 m, capped at full health. A living enemy within 10 m of the fire, or pursuing/returning anywhere in its area, blocks healing, interaction and travel. Unsafe source fires have no hover or click interaction. Discovered destinations remain selectable regardless of enemies there; source safety is rechecked before travel commits. Interactive `campfires` reference a fire effect, destination name and clear arrival transform; entering their 3 m radius discovers them. Only Homestead starts unlocked; approaching the clearing camp fire discovers it. Existing saved discoveries survive; source-fire safety still applies. Clicking a safe fire approaches it and opens Travel, listing discovered fires in other areas. E is an assignable action slot. `portalArrival` reserves the paired return portal's home position; keep it and the nearby arrival space clear.

Fire effects can author `emitterHeight` and `intensity` for ground campfires rather than elevated braziers. Primitive `surface: "woodland"` uses the original local soil texture with three rotated/offset world-space samples and smooth, static TSL noise for soil/moss variation. Optional `patches` author worn ground directly in that material: `center: [x,z]` and positive `radius` are in world metres, `color` is a six-digit hex tint, and `strength` is 0–1. Patches blend in list order with irregular feathered edges; they add no geometry or collision. Homestead uses overlapping worn-soil patches around its fire and arrival point.

Homestead and Forest Clearing connect through a reciprocal woodland path. The blockout remains a developer-only area with valid return links; it is not part of the normal outing.

Forest Clearing has one Goblin guarding a chest and rest/travel fire, plus a staff-wielding caster in a separate glade at `[-10, -5]`, off the left side of the woodland approach. The caster is about 10.6 m from the player start and 13.9 m from the camp guard. Its reserved combat ground stays free of small scenery, with a worn-soil patch and trees framing the edge. Its 6 m notice radius requires line of sight; damaging it also alerts it. Beyond 10 m from its camp or player it returns through navigation, remains a campfire threat, and recovers to full health on arrival. Movement, dodging and missed attacks do not alert distant guards. Base player health is 100; ordinary enemies have 200 health and deal 20 direct damage before armor/blocking. Weapon damage, ability scaling and proficiency follow [combat and equipment](EQUIPMENT.md#combat-values).

Typed `chests` reference a placed asset through `prop`, an interaction `position`, a nonnegative integer `scrolls` reward and optional potion, gold and authored equipment rewards. Chests have no enemy eligibility gate. Clicking the clearing chest approaches and opens it within 1.8 m regardless of whether nearby enemies are alive. It scatters two Scrolls of Return, two Health Potions and the unclaimed equipment in its [authored reward list](EQUIPMENT.md#guaranteed-discoveries-and-persistence); each equipment reward is permanently claimed when collected. Its open lid and ground rewards survive travel, death and application relaunch; inventory overflow remains on the ground. Development authoring’s Restart resets the outing while retaining collected character progress. Individual 45-minute active-play renewal restores enemies and chest supplies; only unclaimed equipment is offered again. Chest lid animation remains presentation-owned and excluded from static mesh batching.

## Graveyard Ruins and Graveyard Crypt

The playable route is Homestead ↔ Forest Clearing ↔ Graveyard Ruins, with Graveyard Crypt branching from the ruined chapel. The ruins are temporarily terminal on the main route; their collapsed onward path reserves future woodland expansion without an invalid gate. The crypt returns through its entrance. Both retain the standard area envelope.

Graveyard Ruins frame an open burial court with clustered, locally authored stone headstones, worn grave slabs, copper woodland silhouettes and the chapel arch. Three sword Skeleton Warriors occupy the court. The entrance campfire is discovered on foot and uses the existing healing/travel safety rule. Graveyard Crypt contains a quiet foyer, a burial hall and a reward alcove, with a short return corridor. Two sword warriors and a staff Bone Caster defend the hall; the off-centre tomb blocks movement, melee and bolts. Tall rear masonry and low foreground walls preserve the fixed camera's view. The independently accessible supply chest scatters two Health Potions and two Scrolls of Return. Indoor torches are cosmetic; the crypt has quiet flame ambience and no healing/travel campfire.

New encounter layouts use `enemies: [{ id, position, yaw, kind, rig, loadout }]`. Stable IDs must be unique, cannot use `player` or Object prototype names, and are shared by targeting, simulation, actor presentation, health bars, reward attribution and saved outing snapshots. `kind` selects the existing `raider` or `caster` behavior; `rig` selects `enemy` (Goblin) or `skeleton`. Legacy `enemy`/`caster` spawns are normalized, including absent slots; do not mix them with `enemies`. Authored packs use small starting cooldown offsets and collision-resolved local separation; planted attacks retain their pose and location.

Chests open without defeating enemies; legacy guard authoring fields are removed. Optional `potions` and existing `scrolls` author supply quantities. The Clearing retains its existing first-collection equipment claims. Skeletons use the current normal humanoid gold-drop rules; the crypt supply chest explicitly disables gold and equipment rewards. Enemy/chest/drop state and individual renewal deadlines now survive application relaunch alongside character progress and campfire discoveries.

`primitive.surface: "stone"` uses the original painterly stone source through shared TSL triplanar sampling. `primitive.kind: "headstone"` supplies a locally authored rounded, bevelled silhouette. `visibility: "lighting-only"` is reserved for cutaway ceiling geometry: it does not enter gameplay camera passes, remains opaque in shadows and probe captures, and participates in lighting fingerprints. Do not mark it transient or disable object visibility. See [interior lighting](LIGHTING.md#interior-cutaways).

## Woodland color composition

Woodland primitives may author `paths: [{ points: [[x,z], ...], width, strength }]`. Width is the full trail width in metres; strength is 0–1. Use 2–32 finite points and no consecutive duplicate points. Terrain blends one continuous distance field along each centerline, with restrained irregular edge breakup; it exposes the existing earth material rather than painting a color overlay. Grass uses the same centerlines for clearance. Circular patches remain useful for accumulated litter, rock contacts and broad camp/glade ground, but should not define a trail.

Woodland ground patches optionally select `layer: "earth" | "litter" | "rocky-soil"` to blend a coordinated material rather than only a color. Existing color patches retain their behavior; centers/radii remain world metres, strength remains 0–1, and order controls overlap. Material sampling belongs to `src/rendering/woodland-ground.ts`; area data owns placement. The development-only Woodland showcase uses explicit bounded patches and four selected Clearing placements from [the environment manifest](../assets/textures/environment/manifest.json), reviewed from `entrance`. The default area material now uses this coordinated family across Clearing and Homestead; placement transforms, navigation and harvest metadata stay authoritative. See [the showcase workflow](ASSET_PREPARATION.md#optional-scenery-and-surface-studies).

Homestead and Forest Clearing use the weathered autumn palette in [Art Direction](ART_DIRECTION.md). Keep copper/rust/ochre in broad foliage clusters, darker olive in supporting masses, and burgundy sparse. Stable colors belong to the prepared asset family, not random per-instance or per-face tinting. Keep soil warm brown, use subdued autumn staining near tree edges, and preserve quiet paths and combat spaces. Color iterations retain transforms, collision, reserved regions and scatter seeds/counts. Technical blockout and movement trials retain their diagnostic colors.

## Fast iteration

Prerequisites: the project's Node version, installed npm dependencies, `agent-browser` on PATH with its browser installed, and Python 3 with Pillow (already required by asset preparation). Use `brew install agent-browser` and `agent-browser install` if needed. Prepared character and Mixamo art are required for playable verification.

1. `npm run levels:dev -- --area=clearing` starts an owned loopback Vite server and isolated headless browser. The preview requires native WebGPU and defaults to FSR Temporal; there is no reconstruction or backend fallback. `--renderer=webgpu` is a compatibility argument; other backends are rejected.
2. Edit area JSON. Vite hot updates preserve renderer/cache, preview camera and graphics settings. Content changes reset temporal history and encounter pose. Frozen previews retain the selected target and zoom. No production build, browser restart or Blender export is needed.
3. Use Area/View, Play/Freeze and Guides controls, or `npm run levels:capture -- --area=clearing`.
4. Inspect one resulting view locally. Correct a visible concern in the same session. `--view ID` selects another view; `--all` explicitly creates the full contact sheet.
5. Use the fast sanity gate after the area stabilizes and one relevant interaction when behavior changed. Broad smoke flows are targeted checks for concrete functional concerns. Measurements require a specific user request or an evidenced performance defect; routine area completion never benchmarks.
6. `npm run levels:stop` closes only the owned browser/server. Close rendering sessions after review to release the agent GPU slot.

The development-only route is `/?author=levels&area=<id>`. Scene-code changes may reload the page; data edits are the optimized path. Invalid definitions preserve the last valid scene and report the area/object. Invalid JSON syntax also triggers Vite's error overlay. Superseded asynchronous loads are discarded and released. Readiness means the committed revision has loaded and rendered, not merely that requests started.

`window.lanternAuthoring` exposes diagnostics, area selection, viewpoint/zoom, freeze/play, overlays, clean captures and frame settling. It exists only in development authoring sessions. The CLI uses this bridge through agent-browser rather than screen coordinates. No object gizmos, undo or scene-saving endpoint is included.

Captures freeze simulation, effects and actor pose, reset temporal history, then render settling frames. Clean captures omit authoring/game HUD; overview includes guides. Files under ignored `.local/level-design/<area>/<runtime-prefix>-<revision>/` include the requested view and manifest; explicit `--all` adds all gameplay views, overview and contact sheet with content hash, settings, asset readiness, errors and timings. A batch fails if its revision, content hash or runtime identity changes. Missing art labels it **incomplete**. Capturing does not constitute visual approval: the designer must inspect the images.

Warm targets: under 2 s from data update to readiness and under 5 s per clean view. Measure actual results, separate cold asset startup, and report misses rather than hiding them with longer timeouts. The full six-view batch takes longer than one capture. A readiness error or missing character must be resolved before claiming playable/visual completion.

## AI designer checklist and brief

Read this guide and the area brief before editing. Inspect current data. Change one coherent concern and inspect one representative preview; a capture is optional. Compare the result with the intended impression using [the visual review criteria](ART_DIRECTION.md#visual-review), alongside actor visibility, combat ground, entrances/exits, shadows, foreground silhouettes and perimeter seams. Normal gameplay scale is the default. Inspect another view/zoom only when a specific concern needs it. Record the visible outcome briefly; separate review notes, smoke matrices and performance reports are not routine completion requirements.

When using a reference, identify the specific lesson in composition, lighting, material, or motion and how it serves this area's intent. Keep reference use compatible with licensing restrictions; never submit licensed Synty content or local renders to ImageGen.

Copy this brief into an area's task or private review notes:

- **Area / intent:** name and purpose; standard envelope or approved exception.
- **Intended impression:** the player's immediate perception.
- **Composition decision:** landmark framing, visual balance, and where detail concentrates.
- **Distinctive feature:** the memorable choice that gives this area character.
- **Restraint:** what stays quiet to support that choice.
- **Landmark:** focal object, silhouette and location.
- **Encounter:** maneuvering space, enemy spawn and readable contact area.
- **Routes / gates:** approach, exit, optional branch; protected arrival regions.
- **Palette / surfaces:** limited palette and approved asset families.
- **Lighting:** main direction, warm/cool accents, fog and shadow priorities.
- **Acceptance views:** entrance, center, exit and two risk-focused viewpoints.
- **Performance / review:** target preset and machine; latest capture revision; retain/revise rationale.

Licensed sources, GLBs, generated bakes and captures remain private under `.local/` or `public/vendor/`. Never submit Synty models/textures/renders to ImageGen. Original text-prompted textures can follow the existing local projection pipeline. Preview/capture/measurement do not modify build selections, export art, or publish anything.

Environment palette, material treatment, the fixed Golden woodland baseline and local surface preparation follow [the art direction](ART_DIRECTION.md).


## Shop and gold reward metadata

Area definitions can author positive integer `level`; enemy spawns can override `level` and add `rank: "normal" | "elite" | "boss"`, `humanoid: true` and `gold: false` opt-outs. Chest `level` overrides area level and `gold: false` disables its opportunity. This metadata affects the [gold formula](LOOT.md#gold-and-homestead-trading), not combat stats or player progression. Humanoid eligibility is explicit and includes humanoid undead.

Homestead's optional `shop` names a stable `id`, counter `prop`, interaction `position: [x,z]`, and `merchant` with private `model` URL, `position`, `yaw` and display `height`. Counter and merchant share the shop interaction; keep counter collision aligned and its approach clear. The animated merchant stays outside static lighting capture. The counter, clustered supplies and lantern remain authored area scenery under shared Golden lighting.

## Authored precipitation and shallow water

`effects.weather: { kind: "rain" }` marks an area eligible for the shared adventure weather. Homestead, Forest Clearing and Graveyard Ruins participate; Graveyard Crypt and diagnostic areas do not. Optional `shelters` retain `{ center, radius }` circles, or use `{ center, halfSize: [x,z], yaw? }` for oriented rectangular cover. `restoredOnly` activates cover only for a restored Homestead appearance. Clearing retains its existing cover; Homestead uses the repaired canvas footprint, with no protection from the ruined cover. Shelter exposure feathers over 0.5 m outside the roof edge and is shared by rain, contacts, ground wetting and listener audio.

`src/gameplay/weather.ts` owns dry → gathering → shower → clearing. Each cycle samples 18–32 dry minutes, 1–3 shower minutes and peak intensity 0.65–1.0; gathering/clearing each fade for 15 seconds. Its separate saved random stream does not consume loot rolls. Active play advances the schedule indoors too; menus, loading, hidden windows and defeat stop it, while short combat hit pause does not. Outdoor travel and save/reload retain the event, with no offline progress. Version 10 character saves include phase, elapsed time, sampled timings/intensity, generator state and wetness; older saves initialize dry weather without discarding progress.

Rain wetness rises at intensity/60 per active second and dries at 1/300 per second after rain stops. Exposed woodland ground combines at most 0.6 of this contribution with permanent authored/bank wetness using the maximum. Shelter exposure stays static; there is no prop-wide wetting, growing puddles or gameplay penalty. The weather uniform adds no render pass or texture upload. Authoring's Weather preview provides Live, Dry, Gathering rain, Shower and Clearing rain plus ground wetness; these overrides never enter character saves. The development-only `window.lanternWeather` bridge exposes the same temporary preview and status.

 Water definitions retain stable IDs, dimensions, flow, optional `yaw` and `shallow`. Optional `preset` selects `stream`, `pond` or `puddle`; `boundary` is a simple polygon of 3–32 normalized local X/Z points in [-1, 1], and `currents` contains up to 32 `{ position, direction }` samples in that same frame, with direction components in [-1, 1]. Missing fields retain procedural footprints and uniform current. `src/levels/water.ts` owns footprint coverage for material masks, wet banks, rain contacts and player crossings. Clearing authors one continuous curved stream across the woodland side, with a shallow path ford, wider gravel bends and rock-shaped currents. The isolated bank puddle is retired. Ordinary grass roots and ground coverage exclude that same water footprint while retaining the seeded placements outside it. The crossing retains ordinary movement speed; shared bed/bank geometry supplies the lower collision/navigation ground described below. `GroundPatch.wetness` in 0–1 provides bounded material darkening/roughness response. Rain uses camera-local bounded streak/contact pools, flat-ground area data and shared wind; no per-drop raycasts or second render graph. Area replacement retires the field, water textures and geometry.

Water uses shared ripple normals, phased flow-map sampling, restrained GPU vertex waves and a physical node material with water IOR, clearcoat reflection, angle/depth-dependent transparency and rougher foam. It renders through the existing native WebGPU pipeline and Golden environment; shared planar scene reflections are owned by the graph, with no refraction pass. Puddles stay flat when authored. Bank wetness packs into the existing ground-coverage texture’s green channel, staying within WebGPU’s default sampled-texture limit, and augments authored `GroundPatch.wetness`; water and wet ground remain when Weather Effects is Off. The existing gait phase and actual player displacement drive pooled crossing splashes and short wakes, with no response while paused, stationary, teleporting or above the surface. Rain contacts use the same bounded ripple/drop pools and shelter exclusions.

Physical damage to living enemies adds moderate crimson droplets and short-lived stains; skeletons, blocked hits and periodic damage retain their existing feedback. Caster bolts use cyan tapered ribbons and confirmed-contact splashes. Combat outcomes and projectile lifetimes stay simulation-owned. All fluid feedback is transient and clears on reset/area replacement; no saved fluid state, persistent accumulation, additional fluid-feedback scene captures, refraction passes or fluid solvers are introduced. Water reflections use the shared graph described below. Particle Effects controls decorative emission while confirmed combat bursts retain their timing and counts.

Optional `channel` supplies 2–24 metre-based local sections `{ x, z, width, depth }`, strictly ordered from `-width/2` to `width/2`, and a positive optional `bankWidth`. Smooth interpolation owns both the water footprint/depth and the carved visual bed. `src/levels/water-terrain.ts` replaces the authored terrain box's top with a triangulated channel cutout and a bounded bed/bank strip; the same clipped ground triangles feed collision and navigation, so characters descend into the bed and step back onto the banks. Optional `obstacles` supplies up to 16 local-metre `{ position, radius }` footprints, shaping surface coverage, local current and foam around rocks. Relocated trees retain IDs and matching collision proxies; resource/chest identities, gates and combat spaces remain intact. Contact ripples use soft broken crests with varied orientation and quieter short wakes, rather than uniform bright rings.

### Reusable water surfaces

Every body can author `height` (default 0.04 m, or 0.015 m for puddles), `depth` (default 0.15 m, or 0.05 m for puddles), `shorelineWidth`, and `surface` overrides for `shallowColor`, `deepColor`, `roughness`, `normalStrength`, `reflectionStrength` and `reflection` (`scene` or `environment`). Fields belong to the water definition, not an area ID. Streams use channel sections; ponds/puddles can use ellipse or polygon footprints with default or explicit depth. `waterTerrain`/`waterGround` share generated beds/banks between rendering and physics for authored terrain boxes; other terrain can supply `traversal.ground` explicitly. Ground replaces the old flat collision floor and supports negative heights, grounded arrivals and below-zero loot.

The shared WebGPU graph owns one half-scene-resolution opaque reflection pass per active water elevation. Bodies at the same level share it; inactive elevations retire with their surface lifetimes. Reflected cameras clip at the water plane and preserve Golden lighting. Projection/distortion and reflected depth blend real scenery/character reflections with the visible bed and environment lighting. No material owns a renderer or a separate render graph. Setting scene reflection strength to zero or selecting environment reflection omits that capture.

Gait-driven water feedback tracks each visible character independently. Feet below the surface produce splashes at the body's water level; stationary, paused, dead, hidden and teleporting actors do not emit crossing feedback. Rain emission/contact height follows the local scene ground or water level. Movement remains ordinary shallow wading, without swimming, slowing, persistent fluid simulation or saved water state.
