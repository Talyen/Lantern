# Runtime reference

Detailed owner contracts. Start with [task routing](ARCHITECTURE.md#task-routing), then read the affected section and its source owners/consumers.

## Owners and data flow

- `src/entry.ts` selects explicit development labs or the application Title/Play flow. `src/application.ts` owns four slots and shared services; `session/session.ts` creates and disposes one selected gameplay session. Explicit level authoring/render inspection retains a disposable fixture world. Legacy art/renderer URLs enter normal Title; explicit authoring/render inspection bypasses selection. Escape opens Options during play.
- `src/gameplay/adventure.ts` owns continuing character progress, discovered campfires, versioned character save, saved outing encounter/drop/chest snapshots and shared fire-safety decisions, cast clock and single return-portal link. `src/gameplay/inventory.ts` owns item transactions, supply transfers and safe equipment displacement, and preserves the public inventory API; `src/ui/adventure.ts` presents Inventory and Travel; `src/ui/loot.ts` projects name-only labels and selection; `src/rendering/adventure.ts` presents drops and portals without deciding their behavior. Its area-owned loot picker retains membership and world bounds; landing/parent transforms and asynchronous prepared-model replacement invalidate those bounds. Drop children remain static between model replacements.
- Adventure delegates wallet/buyback candidates to [shop transactions](../src/gameplay/shop-transactions.ts) and recipe/container candidates to [Homestead transactions](../src/gameplay/homestead-transactions.ts). These owners never save or replace the active character. Adventure checks world eligibility, commits the complete candidate and saves once. Homestead transactions also expose the recipe material counts used by the repair menu and eligibility check. Adventure’s frame update explicitly orders Rested, discovery, healing, enemy rewards, drop collection and return casting, after the death gate.
- Inventory leaf owners separate [catalog/types](../src/gameplay/inventory-catalog.ts), [bag geometry](../src/gameplay/inventory-placement.ts), [equipment queries](../src/gameplay/inventory-equipment.ts) and [untrusted-save validation](../src/gameplay/inventory-validation.ts). They depend directly on each other without importing the transaction facade. Read-only placement and equipment queries accept readonly inventories; receiving items and transaction candidates remain explicitly mutable.
- `src/gameplay/equipment.ts` owns authored item identity, compatible weapon family, shared slots and fixed bonuses; `combat-stats.ts` resolves shared equipment plus the active hand set. Encounter snapshots accepted attack damage, rate and reach; the actor presents the same playback rate. [Combat and equipment](EQUIPMENT.md) owns values, discoveries and resource-cap rules.
- `src/gameplay/encounter.ts` owns plain numeric actor transforms (including ground height), equipment-derived player health, 200-point enemy health, per-enemy notice/pursuit/return state, contact clocks, cooldowns, interruptions and outcomes. It imports neither three.js nor browser globals.
- `src/session/session.ts` coordinates startup, simulation, pause gates, resets and the frame loop. `session/diagnostics.ts` serializes live coordinator state for development authoring and explicitly selects bounded report fields; report export never consumes the full character snapshot. `session/menu-controller.ts` owns menu exclusivity, toggling and the shared menu pause query. `session/interaction-actions.ts` executes reached world targets, including live campfire travel checks and successful return-portal consumption; area preparation and commit remain in the coordinator. `session/area-change.ts` owns the shared travel/refresh request types. `src/levels/builder.ts` owns area geometry, optional scenery and firelights, updating hit tilt only for struck trees until their authored pose is restored; the graphics owner applies shared area lighting and atmosphere; `actors.ts` owns character instances, mixers, actions and timing.
- `src/session/input.ts` exposes movement, pointer aim, attack, dodge and menu requests; `camera.ts` owns follow, zoom, projection, inspection/authoring views and camera snapshots restored after area refreshes. `pointer-aim.ts` retains the last displayed unjittered camera for world and enemy picking.
- `src/session/world-interactions.ts` retains area-owned scene target descriptors and synchronous query storage, checking live gameplay eligibility on every query. Triangle picking first tests eligible objects, then checks scenery and actor occlusion only as far as the nearest visible target. Area replacement releases these references. `click-approach.ts` owns one active loot/object route, its refresh/stall clocks and cancellation. `session/gathering.ts` coordinates gathering selection, presentation-only tools, motion contacts and resource collision updates, reusing numeric occupant records for synchronous regrowth checks. Gathering threat eligibility and depletion remain in `gameplay/harvesting.ts`; rewards and XP remain in Adventure. The ground-loot owner defines the shared landing duration and pickup radius used by simulation, routing and presentation.
- `src/session/equipment-sets.ts` prepares both typed weapon sets before replacement, activates retained attachments and supplies read-only ability timings refreshed only after successful equipment preparation; `rendering/equipment.ts` owns their resource release. `session/inventory.ts` owns equipment preparation gates, loadout synchronization and inventory commits with their menu/audio updates. Character progress commits only after preparation succeeds. `session/combat.ts` owns player ability, swap and dodge commands, shield input release and actor timing inputs; the coordinator consumes their events and keeps area/frame ownership.
- `src/session/frame-loop.ts` owns frame requests, elapsed time, hidden suspension, paused settling and authoring frame waits. Closing the loop cancels its request and rejects outstanding waits; stale area requests cannot claim completion after settling. `src/rendering/frame-pacer.ts` gates frames against the chosen FPS cap. The coordinator advances simulation and presentation only through the loop's frame callback. Input owns abortable canvas/window listeners and clears held state when disposed.
- `assets/motion-profiles.json` owns stable Mixamo source IDs, trims and contact/release markers; `src/animation/combat-animations.ts` owns compatible weapon-profile selection and lazy clip loading. Clip wrappers and action clocks are independent; cached keyframe tracks are read-only and shared, including masked shield actions. Bow draw/release joins are cached by their source URL pair and share immutable synthesized tracks across prepared profiles. Staff pose preparation copies only tracks it edits. See [animation review](ANIMATIONS.md). Each actor has its own compatible catalog: Erika Archer for the player, Goblin D Shareyko in the Clearing, and Skeleton 01 in the graveyard areas. `assets/playable-characters.json` owns one authored model URL per actor, heights and compatible catalogs.
- `src/audio/` owns Web Audio playback, sound preferences and event-to-cue presentation. Encounter and Adventure expose numeric action/result events; animation replays never trigger sounds. The shared library remains read-only and optimized recordings are private under `public/vendor/audio/`. See [gameplay sound](AUDIO.md).
- `src/ui/hud.ts` presents animated health/mana orbs, recently damaged enemy health, results and asset errors. `options.ts` presents Graphics and Sound and opens the Keybindings editor; rendering, audio and input preference owners parse and persist their respective preferences. See [graphics settings](GRAPHICS.md), [gameplay sound](AUDIO.md) and [input ownership](#action-bar-and-input-ownership). Gameplay loads coherent equipment motion profiles directly; motion selection belongs in the development comparison lab. UI invokes coordinator callbacks for input clearing and graphics updates; development authoring owns Restart and inspection controls.
- `src/rendering/renderer.ts` creates only a native WebGPU renderer and disables the pinned three.js internal fallback before initialization. `graphics.ts` applies settings, owns postprocessing and effects cleanup, and records frame measurements. `graphics-settings.ts` owns defaults and settings parsing. `area-lighting.ts` owns cached procedural PMREM skies and static irradiance probe preparation, using the globally bounded leased cache in `lighting-cache.ts`. `levels/lighting.ts` resolves the single Golden preset into one committed lighting definition for all consumers. Area transitions prepare lighting before commit; old lighting survives failed or superseded loads. Rendering receives scene and lighting references; it does not import UI or decide combat outcomes.
- `src/rendering/webgpu-pipeline.ts` owns the WebGPU render graph, scene/output dimensions and FSR reactivity. `fsr-temporal.ts` bridges the pinned FSR package to r186 pipeline callbacks and prepares its adapted accumulation shader (`fsr-accumulate.wgsl`) before startup. Converged stationary history survives current-phase clipping; motion, depth rejection, shading changes and reactivity restore clipping. FSR motion fades thin-feature locks and reduces accumulation from the package's 24-sample stationary limit to 8, with at least 1/8 current-frame contribution at full motion (0.5 render texels/frame). The package retains pass bindings, dispatch and resource ownership; integration or shader compilation failures stop startup with an actionable error. Pipeline preparation awaits the version-pinned fullscreen graph through renderer compilation before treating FSR as ready. `effects.ts` owns procedural effects and their resources.
- `src/assets/asset-library.ts` owns shared library resources, prepared node materials and releasable instances. `src/assets/asset-assembly.ts` constructs exported hierarchies with borrowed art; `src/rendering/asset-lods.ts` applies their authored screen-height thresholds independently of loading. `src/assets/resource-ownership.ts` retains concrete mesh, line and texture types after the same three.js constructor checks, and separates instance disposal (native mesh bindings and explicitly owned skeletons) from shared geometry/material/texture disposal. Removing a mesh from the scene alone leaves its r186 native render objects retained; area, equipment, drop, projectile and lighting-capture owners explicitly release instances while preserving shared art. `src/labs/animations/animation-lab.ts` owns motion comparison using two instances of the same WebGPU pipeline, independent perspective cameras, native renderers and temporal histories, displayed side by side with linked orbit controls.
- `electron/main.cjs` serves the built renderer through the secure local lantern://app scheme with sandboxing, context isolation, and no Node integration. It supplies the initial display refresh rate for the default FPS cap.

## Area replacement

`src/levels/` owns typed area definitions, validation, seeded decoration and shared runtime construction. See [level design](LEVEL_DESIGN.md). Named area-change requests distinguish travel from appearance refresh; both prepare resources before commit. Area construction uses one guarded cleanup path across initial allocation, asynchronous loading and final batching; pending texture callbacks settle before failed candidates release their resources. The coordinator's area candidate owns geometry, movement and prepared lighting until eligibility and precommit callbacks succeed, releasing rejected, superseded or throwing candidates exactly once. `gameplay/area-encounter.ts` owns encounter restoration at commit: authored enemy identities and layouts remain current while defeated enemies, health and player cooldowns survive travel. Adventure owns the inactive area snapshots and outing state. Area replacement releases its effects/instances and keeps renderer/shared asset caches; gameplay owns numeric boundaries, spawn/reset and gate state. Development-only authoring consumes this same runtime through a narrow automation bridge.

## Simulation and pause

`src/gameplay/encounter.ts` remains the public simulation entry point and owns creation, reset and frame ordering. `encounter-model.ts` owns shared state, events and tuning; `encounter-player.ts` owns equipment commits, buffered actions and player contacts; `encounter-enemies.ts` owns pursuit, leash recovery and enemy windups; `encounter-projectiles.ts` owns swept collision; `encounter-damage.ts` owns shared damage, interruption and finite damage-credit budgets. `action-commit.ts` owns exactly-once spending and shared Ultimate readiness; `encounter-effects.ts` owns numeric Poison and rain pulses. Internal modules consume the model directly rather than importing the entry point, so their dependencies remain acyclic. Buffered inputs are a discriminated union: abilities require an ability ID and dodges require their accepted direction.

Simulation operations mutate an explicitly owned encounter: create, reset, attack, and step. Updates return ordered animation, hit, label and outcome events. Presentation synchronizes transforms, then consumes events; rendering never decides health or victory. Player contacts resolve before movement and enemy attacks. Player input, movement and cooldowns advance once per frame; each authored enemy then advances its own AI. Clip durations remain inputs rather than render-owned clocks inside simulation.

Pausing, inspection, equipment loading and graph preparation stop simulation. Hidden/minimized gameplay clears held input and suspends simulation/rendering without catch-up time; visible unfocused play retains its existing behavior. Frozen views and menus draw 64 settling frames, then sleep until input, camera, settings, resize, scene changes or an explicit authoring frame request. HTML animation remains independent. Hidden Electron inspection opts in through `inspection=render`. The coordinator combines menu, inspection and preparation gates. Developer Restart resets encounter state, session chests/drops/portals, resource depletion, animation actions, effects and temporal history together. Adventure and Harvesting reset their session state while collected items, claims, skills, shelter, stash and Rested remain character progress. Camera follow preserves a fixed 35-degree downward pitch and 45-degree azimuth, with saved Camera Distance multipliers Default (0.6) and Far (0.78) mapped to reciprocal zoom, and eases only its focus point. This is an ARPG-style approximation, not a verified camera specification from another game. Temporal sampling offsets are render-only; exactly one temporal resolve owns jitter, and the unjittered camera is restored before the next gameplay frame. Avoid moving runtime rules into a renderer or Options callback.

## Reconstruction and presentation

FSR keeps presentation at native physical drawable resolution and reduces only scene buffers according to Resolution Quality. The beauty pass omits unused normals; the reactive mask uses a single-channel half-float target and full-screen composites omit depth attachments. AO writes only view normals and depth in an independent opaque geometry pass and the native material `getAO` context hook, occluding indirect diffuse/specular light while preserving direct lighting and emission. AO precedes reconstruction. FSR sharpening is part of reconstruction; restrained DOF follows the resolved output with a jitter-corrected bilateral depth guide, followed by bloom, grading, ACES and one sRGB conversion. FSR reactive coverage comes from alpha compositing in the beauty MRT, avoiding a second fully shaded world pass. Static ground patch/path/wetness weights become a 512-square RGBA lookup during area preparation instead of hundreds of shader expressions. Startup FSR failure produces an actionable error; runtime errors after successful rendering remain visible errors. Simulation remains independent of resolution quality.

## Pointer aim

Pointer coordinates stay in input; the coordinator reprojects them every active frame using the last displayed unjittered camera and the player's ground-height plane. Simulation receives an optional numeric aim point, applies facing after movement, and ignores points within 0.15 m. Pointer facing takes precedence over movement facing while free; attacks commit facing at acceptance, hit locks hold it, and dodges commit WASD direction or stationary pointer aim. Clearing input also clears aim, so menus and transitions require a fresh pointer event. Cleared or suppressed keyboard holds require a fresh press; autorepeat cannot restore them. Compatible forward/backward/strafe loops follow actual collision-resolved displacement relative to aim. Normalized gait phases align foot contacts; blocking uses directional walking legs beneath its shield pose.

## Camera follow

Camera follow owns a ground-plane anchor with a 0.15 m radial dead zone and a separately eased height. Actual collision-resolved displacement produces 0.20 seconds of look-ahead, capped at 0.65 m; anchor and lead ease exponentially at 8 per second. Lead is suppressed inside the dead zone. Pointer turning never moves the camera. Restart, area arrival and inspection return reset follow state while retaining the player's chosen Camera Distance; pauses and fixed authoring views discard velocity samples without moving the view. The fixed offset remains owned by `src/session/projection.ts`.

## Navigation ownership

`src/gameplay/movement.ts` owns navcat generation/path queries and Rapier grounded collision/line-of-sight behind a numeric simulation interface. Area data supplies explicit proxies and optional slope triangles plus stable harvestable trunk proxies, independent of asset availability. Tree-state changes commit collision immediately; one microtask builds and submits their final navigation geometry to the worker, coalescing session depletion applied during arrival. Stale worker revisions remain ineligible. The coordinator replaces/disposes this adapter with the area and passes desired movement through it before presenting transforms.

Actor collision lives in `src/gameplay/movement-actors.ts`. The encounter synchronizes area-present actors before queries and after contacts, disables dead proxies, and applies only returned numeric movement. New and replacement actor poses ground before queries, including renewed enemies that retain an existing collision ID. Walking slides around living bodies without pushing them. Existing overlaps may separate through outward movement. Body proxies never participate in scenery-only attack visibility, projectile-wall, ground-loot or resource queries. Attack and hit-reaction poses remain planted; animation uses resolved movement rather than a requested route.

Dodge ignores bodies during travel but reserves a clear capsule landing, shortening its existing 2.4 m travel budget when needed. The adapter prepares a grounded terrain trajectory and replays it across frames; scenery changes replan only the remaining budget. Enemy movement respects the temporary landing reservation. Landing, death, restart and disposal release it. A blocked roll retains its existing animation, 0.45-second duration, immunity and cooldown; it never gains distance or pushes another actor.

`src/gameplay/movement-steering.ts` owns transient tactical claims and Navcat local-boundary/adaptive avoidance proposals, not another position simulation. Melee approach positions use twelve candidates at 1.35 m; ready enemies may all attack without an attacker quota. Ranged enemies hold any valid firing position, even near the player, and seek reachable visible positions on 4.5/5.5 m rings only when obstructed or out of range. Candidate visibility uses Rapier's real standing height, rather than the voxel navmesh height, so low cover cannot produce a false firing goal. Goals refresh after 250 ms, 40 cm target movement, invalidation or a 0.5-second stall. Stable-ID claim preparation does not reorder authored combat contacts. Far-side melee claims use a reachable circling waypoint on a 2.4 m ring when angular error exceeds 20 degrees, so desired movement does not cut through the front rank. Nearby actor and wall avoidance uses a 2 m query range, 0.375 m radius and 0.6-second prediction horizon; Rapier-resolved velocity feeds back into steering. Failed ranged searches wait; melee retains valid approach routing. No post-movement separation pushes remain.

Thrust may close an aimed visible enemy gap by up to 0.75 m, during the final half of preparation before its contact marker. Direction and distance snapshot on acceptance; near targets and empty swings stay planted. The lunge stops at the first wall/body contact and resolves before damage, without homing, sideways sliding, immunity or target displacement. Existing damage, reach, commitment, unlock and attack-rate scaling remain authoritative in [Equipment](EQUIPMENT.md#sword-and-bow-mastery). Dodge cancels its remaining movement. Lunge, goal, velocity and reservation state is transient and does not extend the save format. Pause/loading/hit-stop freeze simulation.

## Vegetation and tree presentation

`src/rendering/vegetation.ts` owns area-local grass/low-plant interaction uniforms and material variants. The clearing coordinator stages numeric foot positions after movement resolves; the effects owner consumes them only when rendering advances. Four stable slots cover the living player and three nearest living, visible enemies. Current/previous influence data, a 0.12-second trailing position and a 0.5 m trail cap support bounded node deformation without a persistent field or per-blade simulation. Pause/hit-stop freezes interaction recovery, and presentation reset/area replacement clears both histories.

The area builder resolves canonical bush/fern or explicit placement vegetation profiles, adds static root/height/footprint attributes, and preserves compatible instancing. Source library geometry/materials are borrowed; area-owned attribute copies and material variants are restored and released before borrowed instances. Existing `foliage` wind remains separate from proximity eligibility. Vegetation does not affect gameplay queries or persistence.

`src/rendering/tree-felling.ts` owns active cosmetic falls, prepared per-tree fade variants and their cleanup. Harvesting commits rewards/depletion and navigation removes the trunk immediately; `fellTree` supplies only the new visual reaction. Snapshot restoration bypasses animation, while renewal resets the original pose and coverage. Native node alpha hashing handles disappearance without an additional pass, and standing geometry remains eligible for lighting preparation. Falls run on gameplay time and never enter saved state or request lighting/navigation work.

## Portal presentation

`src/rendering/portal.ts` owns the generic gold/amber portal and bounded decal; the retained analytic swirl uses TSL through the existing graph. Portal effects pause with gameplay; portal destinations and lifetime belong to Adventure. See [library trials](LIBRARY_TRIALS.md).

## Travel and fire safety

Homestead is a safe area: movement remains active, enemies and combat outcomes are absent, and safe healing fires restore 3% maximum health per gameplay second within 3 m. Campfire interaction, healing and Continue checkpoints require no living enemy within 10 m and no pursuing/returning enemy anywhere in the area. Unsafe campfires are excluded from hover and click targets: they have no interaction contour, cursor or prompt, and clicking them follows ordinary ground-click behavior without a message. An approach to a fire that becomes unsafe cancels silently. Campfire Travel requires a safe source fire but ignores enemies at the destination: every discovered destination remains selectable. Travel also requires a living player within 3 m of the source fire, no active Return cast and a discovered destination; source safety and eligibility are rechecked before commit. Campfire menus pause simulation; a two-second Scroll of Return cast runs alongside combat and movement, with death taking precedence. Travel commits only after destination resources and eligibility checks succeed, retaining player health and inactive enemy health/defeat plus ground drops. Entry clears transient attack actions rather than replaying attacks across scenes. Return departures retain ground height for portal placement, interaction and arrival. A portal pair survives campfire travel, Return to Title and application relaunch in the saved outing. Returning through its home end consumes it only after destination commit; death recovery and development authoring’s Restart clear it. Death offers Return Home with collected scrolls retained.

## Save recovery

Character revision 10 adds shared outdoor weather to the outing snapshot. Legacy characters migrate with dry weather, preserving their inventory, progression and outing. Weather resumes its saved phase, wetness and random state without offline progress; malformed weather uses the existing whole-snapshot rejection and backup recovery. See [weather ownership](LEVEL_DESIGN.md#authored-precipitation-and-shallow-water).

**Continue versus Restart:** normal application startup or page reload opens Title; Continue restores the selected saved outing at its safe checkpoint. Only explicit development authoring/render-inspection routes may use `?area=<id>` to override fixture arrival; ordinary area URLs do not bypass selection or redirect fresh starts. Development authoring’s **Restart** deliberately resets enemy/chest/resource state, ground drops, portals and transient combat state while retaining collected fixture progress. Authoring fixtures do not read or write adventure slots. Use Continue for persistence checks and Restart only for a fresh fixture outing.

Normal entry initializes the app and native WebGPU renderer, then opens Title. Play presents exactly four named independent adventures. [Application](../src/application.ts) owns selection, shared Options/keybindings/audio, renderer lifetime and pending saves; [front-end](../src/ui/front-end.ts) owns the adopted [Title/Play screens](ui/concepts/title-play/README.md). [Adventure store](../src/gameplay/adventure-store.ts) receives injected storage and owns `lantern.adventure.1.v1` through `lantern.adventure.4.v1`, each with a version-1 envelope containing immutable adventure identity, name and the revision-10 character payload. Each slot has a validated `.backup`, an authoritative `.identity` record and deduplicated `.unreadable` archives. Unknown or unreadable slots cannot become fresh characters implicitly. Names are trimmed, required, at most 24 Unicode code points and fixed after creation; duplicate names are allowed. `lantern.adventures.selected.v1` is an optional shared preference, not automatic Continue.

`gameplay/character.ts` owns live character fields and non-enumerable derived totals/loadout; `character-save.ts` validates revision 10 and migrates revisions 1–9 without losing equipment, inventory, wallet/buyback, stash, proficiency, discoveries, permanent rewards, restoration or Rested. `gameplay/outing.ts` validates enemy health/finite XP budgets/rewards/deadlines, chests, resources, ground drops/provenance, checkpoint, portal and committed cooldowns. Adventure snapshots those fields together. New Adventure starts fresh at Homestead. Continue places the character at the last safely reached fire with full health, falling back to Homestead if the fire is invalid or unsafe. Transient inputs, preparations, projectiles and unfinished Return casts are cleared; unfinished casts consume no scroll. Closed-app and Title time advance neither renewal nor Rested.

Existing `lantern.character.v1` primary or validated backup migrates into slot 1 as Adventure 1, retaining the entire outing. Legacy bytes remain intact. A verified target write permits `lantern.adventures.legacy-migrated.v1`; a partially completed migration recovers through the slot identity/backup. An occupied first slot is never overwritten, and a durable deletion identity prevents later legacy reimport even without the migration marker.

Save recovery is deliberately invisible. Valid backups recover automatically; damaged bytes are archived before replacement, and failed preservation leaves them untouched. The application retains the latest snapshot for each slot across Return to Title and switching. Failed writes coalesce and retry after 1, 2, 5, 15 and then 30 seconds through `data/retry.ts`. Page exit makes one final synchronous attempt and cancels timers. Return to Title captures the outing and attempts saving before releasing play; failed saving still permits departure with an in-memory snapshot. Closing before persistence recovers can lose pending changes. A slot with no readable primary, backup or known in-memory snapshot stays on Play with a brief message to retry Continue, confirming that saved files were kept; subsequent attempts retry. No save warnings, backup prompts or confirmed unsaved-departure flow are shown.

Confirmed deletion writes a deleted identity before removing primary/backup copies. Recreating a slot uses a new adventure identity. Stored backups must match the current identity, and gameplay callbacks must match both adventure and active session identity; obsolete writes cannot revive deleted/replaced adventures or superseded sessions. Graphics, audio, combat-text preferences and keybindings remain shared. The older `character-persistence.ts` remains the injected single-character boundary used by existing model fixtures; normal application play uses the four-slot store. Persistence diagnostics remain bounded and omit names/payloads.

## Loot ownership

[Ground loot](../src/gameplay/ground-loot.ts) owns drop records, the 0.55-second landing clock, pickup radius and partial collection. Adventure owns identities, reachable landing position/height, pickup target, reward events, XP and saved area outings. Supplies collect within 1.5 m after landing only when a nearby physical route exists; equipment requires an explicit label/object click and never auto-equips. Pickup navigation has an independent path query, leaving enemy pursuit caches intact. Travel, death and application relaunch retain drops in saved area outings. Enemy/chest/resource rewards expire when their individual source renews after 45 active-play minutes; visible or nearby sources and rewards defer renewal. Player-dropped loot remains independent. Dropped supplies wait for departure/re-entry before automatic recollection. The clearing chest scatters two scrolls and two Health Potions on each opening, renewing independently after 45 active-play minutes and offers each unclaimed weapon; collection records permanent claims. Harvesting contacts create Wood, Stone or Iron drops with XP provenance; only successfully collected quantities award gathering XP.

## Smithing and damage types

[Smithing](SMITHING.md) owns the Homestead workshop, recipe/reclamation candidates, saved-XP unlocks and automatic talents. Forging consumes Bag then unlocked Stash materials and places gear in Bag only after two foreground seconds; cancellation and failed placement retain all resources. Reclamation is immediate and permanent, excludes equipped gear and returns matching materials to the source container. Adventure owns station eligibility, atomic publication, stat refresh and autosave.

[Damage vocabulary](../src/gameplay/damage.ts) separates Physical, Burn, Freeze, Poison, Bleed and Nature from statuses. Stun is loss of control, not health damage. Direct-hit armor/blocking still apply across types; Heat Seasoned adds Burn Resistance afterward. Player Staff uses Nature, Bone Caster uses Freeze, goblin fireballs use Burn, and Poison Arrow ticks use Poison. Typed visuals share the native pipeline and bounded particle owners.

## Inventory commits

Menus pause gameplay. Inventory rearrangement, splitting, packing and equipment displacement compute candidates first; asset/motion preparation completes before equipped-state changes commit. Dragging outside drops items onto reachable local ground. A Scroll of Return cast reserves one scroll against dropping until completion or death. Each enemy has a 50% one-scroll reward, 200 health and 20 direct damage. Weapon damage, ability scaling, mastery and resource-cap rules belong to [combat and equipment](EQUIPMENT.md); armor reduces all direct hits independently of shield blocking. Gold/trading use the [economy owner](#gold-trading-and-merchant-ownership); broader outing balance remains planned. Enemies renew independently with fresh life/reward budgets after 45 active-play minutes, only off-screen and at least 12 m away.

## Rendering contract

Optional outlines are owned by `src/rendering/outlines.ts` within the shared graph. Mesh instances carry actor/prop strength; unregistered terrain, vegetation and effects default to zero. The environment surface family mapping selects solid scenery, and batching keys retain that classification. When enabled, an extra scene MRT attachment records strength with the beauty pass's cutouts, deformation and jitter. Soft inward mask/depth contours darken scene color before FSR, using the same depth and velocity without another geometry pass or temporal filter. Transparent fragments contribute coverage to the shared reactive attachment. Off omits the active outline attachment and contour processing; the existing bounded graph cache may retain a recently used appearance. Toggle commits reset temporal history. Outlines default/reset to Off and persist independently of other graphics choices.

All routes require native WebGPU. There is one visual pipeline in `src/rendering/webgpu-pipeline.ts`; gameplay, authoring and animation comparison use it. FSR Temporal is the sole reconstruction method, with Quality and sharpening 0.50 as fresh/reset defaults. Output follows the physical drawable viewport through devicePixelRatio; FSR choices scale only internal buffers. SMAA/FXAA and the WebGL composer are removed. Unsupported native initialization stops startup with an actionable error; there is no backend substitution. The renderer's r186 `_getFallback` integration and backend compilation-error bridge must be revalidated on three.js upgrades. The bridge rejects shader failures that three.js otherwise logs while resolving `compileAsync`. Depth conversion supports orthographic and perspective cameras. The pinned FSR adapter replaces accumulation and exposure metering: history is converted into the current conditioning/host pre-exposure domain before blending; metering retains the package’s 32×32 sample grid with a cooperative 64-lane reduction. Default color and reactive inputs use scene MRT attachments directly, with the reactive maximum of 0.9 enforced during accumulation. AO/outline composites retain scene-resolution color targets only where needed. The FSR before-render hook rebinds the active graph’s unjittered motion projection every frame; preparation/disposal clears the shared velocity singleton, so binding only during setup is insufficient and causes stationary sampling jitter to become false motion.

Legacy renderer URL parameters are ignored; retired rendering-method, render-scale and volumetric fields are ignored. Settings revision 9 retains the migration from old High/Low quality to independent shadow and particle presets, preserving applicable player preferences. CLI renderer overrides accept only the compatibility value `webgpu`. `scripts/check-rendering.mjs`, included in the handoff gate, prevents application WebGL support, legacy shader hooks, direct scene rendering and additional render-graph owners. Third-party packages may still contain unused backend code.

`src/rendering/surface-detail.ts` owns material-only 16× filtering, resolution-derived mip bias with one additional finer mip and bounded POM sampling. The bias is `clamp(log2(scene/output), −1, 0) − 1`: Native −1, Quality approximately −1.585, Balanced approximately −1.766 and Performance −2. General foliage wind supplies previous-frame deformation to FSR, including zero elapsed time while paused; grass and reactive vegetation retain their existing previous-position handling. Pipeline contexts supply per-graph bias and the compiled Texture Depth choice; cached On/Off graphs have independent uniforms/history. Geometry AO and irradiance preparation omit parallax; beauty uses it when enabled. Material coordinates use physical surface gradients for orthographic/perspective cameras and preserve geometric depth/velocity/shadows. Loaded standard materials retain authored maps; prepared environment materials read height/cavity/parallax-mask fields from version-3 packed channels (height/roughness/metalness/eligibility in metallic-roughness RGBA, cavity in normal alpha) or legacy version-1/2 sidecars, recorded in lighting fingerprints and build dependencies. `woodland-ground.ts` owns coherent ground projections and normal/height-aware blending. Recipe and private-bake ownership stays in the environment manifest/exporter.

## Asset and resource ownership

## Input buffering

Input buffering holds one attack, ability, dodge or weapon-swap request for 150 ms and clears with menus, travel, death and reset. A buffered attack starts at its recovery unlock; only the remaining portion of that frame advances its contacts and new cooldown. When an existing dodge ends within that frame, its remaining travel completes before the buffered action starts. Ordinary movement after an attack uses only the frame time after recovery. Enemy damage does not interrupt player attacks, movement or held defense. An available dodge cancels preparation/recovery immediately, including hit pause. Before the first swing/release, cancellation spends no attack resources or cooldown; after commitment there is no refund. Riposte commits on its successful prevention. Already released projectiles and rain continue after dodge. Safe and cleared areas retain basic attacks.
