# Runtime reference

Detailed owner contracts. Start with [task routing](ARCHITECTURE.md#task-routing), then read the affected section and its source owners/consumers.

## Owners and data flow

- `src/entry.ts` selects the development-only character gallery/animation comparison lab or normal play (starting midway along Forest Clearing’s approach). Legacy art/renderer URLs load the clearing without opening Options; Escape opens it.
- `src/gameplay/adventure.ts` owns continuing character progress, discovered campfires, versioned character save, per-session encounter/drop/chest snapshots and shared fire-safety decisions, cast clock and single return-portal link. `src/gameplay/inventory.ts` owns footprints, item instances, spatial placement, supply transfers and safe equipment displacement; `src/ui/adventure.ts` presents Inventory and Travel; `src/ui/loot.ts` projects name-only labels and selection; `src/rendering/adventure.ts` presents drops and portals without deciding their behavior. Its area-owned loot picker retains membership and world bounds; landing/parent transforms and asynchronous prepared-model replacement invalidate those bounds. Drop children remain static between model replacements.
- `src/gameplay/equipment.ts` owns authored item identity, compatible weapon family, shared slots and fixed bonuses; `combat-stats.ts` resolves shared equipment plus the active hand set. Encounter snapshots accepted attack damage, rate and reach; the actor presents the same playback rate. [Combat and equipment](EQUIPMENT.md) owns values, discoveries and resource-cap rules.
- `src/gameplay/encounter.ts` owns plain numeric actor transforms (including ground height), equipment-derived player health, 200-point enemy health, per-enemy notice/pursuit/return state, contact clocks, cooldowns, interruptions and outcomes. It imports neither three.js nor browser globals.
- `src/clearing/clearing.ts` coordinates startup, simulation, pause gates, resets and the frame loop. `clearing/menu-controller.ts` owns menu exclusivity, toggling and the shared menu pause query. `clearing/interaction-actions.ts` executes reached world targets, including live campfire travel checks and successful return-portal consumption; area preparation and commit remain in the coordinator. `clearing/area-change.ts` owns the shared travel/refresh request types. `src/levels/builder.ts` owns area geometry, optional scenery and firelights, updating hit tilt only for struck trees until their authored pose is restored; the graphics owner applies shared area lighting and atmosphere; `actors.ts` owns character instances, mixers, actions and timing.
- `src/clearing/input.ts` exposes movement, pointer aim, attack, dodge and menu requests; `camera.ts` owns follow, zoom, projection and inspection views. `pointer-aim.ts` retains the last displayed unjittered camera for world and enemy picking.
- `src/clearing/world-interactions.ts` retains area-owned scene target descriptors and synchronous query storage, checking live gameplay eligibility on every query. Triangle picking first tests eligible objects, then checks scenery and actor occlusion only as far as the nearest visible target. Area replacement releases these references. `click-approach.ts` owns one active loot/object route, its refresh/stall clocks and cancellation. `clearing/gathering.ts` coordinates gathering selection, presentation-only tools, motion contacts and resource collision updates, reusing numeric occupant records for synchronous regrowth checks. Gathering threat eligibility and depletion remain in `gameplay/harvesting.ts`; rewards and XP remain in Adventure. Adventure owns the shared loot landing duration and pickup radius used by simulation, routing and presentation.
- `src/clearing/equipment-sets.ts` prepares both typed weapon sets before replacement, activates retained attachments and supplies read-only ability timings refreshed only after successful equipment preparation; `rendering/equipment.ts` owns their resource release. `clearing/inventory.ts` owns equipment preparation gates, loadout synchronization and inventory commits with their menu/audio updates. Character progress commits only after preparation succeeds. `clearing/combat.ts` owns player ability, swap and dodge commands, shield input release and actor timing inputs; the coordinator consumes their events and keeps area/frame ownership.
- `src/clearing/frame-loop.ts` owns frame requests, elapsed time, hidden suspension, paused settling and authoring frame waits. Closing the loop cancels its request and rejects outstanding waits; stale area requests cannot claim completion after settling. `src/rendering/frame-pacer.ts` gates frames against the chosen FPS cap. The coordinator advances simulation and presentation only through the loop's frame callback. Input owns abortable canvas/window listeners and clears held state when disposed.
- `assets/motion-profiles.json` owns stable Mixamo source IDs, trims and contact/release markers; `src/animation/combat-animations.ts` owns compatible weapon-profile selection and lazy clip loading. Clip wrappers and action clocks are independent; cached keyframe tracks are read-only and shared, including masked shield actions. Bow draw/release joins are cached by their source URL pair and share immutable synthesized tracks across prepared profiles. Staff pose preparation copies only tracks it edits. See [animation review](ANIMATIONS.md). Each actor has its own compatible catalog: Paladin J Nordstrom for the player and Goblin D Shareyko for the enemy. `assets/playable-characters.json` owns one authored model URL per actor, heights and compatible catalogs.
- `src/audio/` owns Web Audio playback, sound preferences and event-to-cue presentation. Encounter and Adventure expose numeric action/result events; animation replays never trigger sounds. The shared library remains read-only and optimized recordings are private under `public/vendor/audio/`. See [gameplay sound](AUDIO.md).
- `src/ui/hud.ts` presents animated health/mana orbs, recently damaged enemy health, results and asset errors. `options.ts` presents Graphics and Sound and opens the Keybindings editor; rendering, audio and input preference owners parse and persist their respective preferences. See [graphics settings](GRAPHICS.md), [gameplay sound](AUDIO.md) and [input ownership](#action-bar-and-input-ownership). Gameplay loads coherent equipment motion profiles directly; motion selection belongs in the development comparison lab. UI invokes coordinator callbacks for input clearing and graphics updates; development authoring owns Restart and inspection controls.
- `src/rendering/renderer.ts` creates only a native WebGPU renderer and disables the pinned three.js internal fallback before initialization. `graphics.ts` applies settings, owns postprocessing and effects cleanup, and records frame measurements. `graphics-settings.ts` owns defaults and settings parsing. `area-lighting.ts` owns cached procedural PMREM skies and static irradiance probe preparation, using the globally bounded leased cache in `lighting-cache.ts`. `levels/lighting.ts` resolves the single Golden preset into one committed lighting definition for all consumers. Area transitions prepare lighting before commit; old lighting survives failed or superseded loads. Rendering receives scene and lighting references; it does not import UI or decide combat outcomes.
- `src/rendering/webgpu-pipeline.ts` owns the WebGPU render graph, scene/output dimensions and FSR reactivity. `fsr-temporal.ts` bridges the pinned FSR package to r186 pipeline callbacks and prepares its adapted accumulation shader (`fsr-accumulate.wgsl`) before startup. Converged stationary history survives current-phase clipping; motion, depth rejection, shading changes and reactivity restore clipping. FSR motion fades thin-feature locks and reduces accumulation from the package's 24-sample stationary limit to 8, with at least 1/8 current-frame contribution at full motion (0.5 render texels/frame). The package retains pass bindings, dispatch and resource ownership; integration or shader compilation failures stop startup with an actionable error. Pipeline preparation awaits the version-pinned fullscreen graph through renderer compilation before treating FSR as ready. `effects.ts` owns procedural effects and their resources.
- `src/assets/asset-library.ts` owns shared library resources, prepared node materials and releasable instances. `src/assets/asset-assembly.ts` constructs exported hierarchies with borrowed art; `src/rendering/asset-lods.ts` applies their authored screen-height thresholds independently of loading. `src/assets/resource-ownership.ts` retains concrete mesh, line and texture types after the same three.js constructor checks, and separates instance disposal (native mesh bindings and explicitly owned skeletons) from shared geometry/material/texture disposal. Removing a mesh from the scene alone leaves its r186 native render objects retained; area, equipment, drop, projectile and lighting-capture owners explicitly release instances while preserving shared art. `src/labs/animations/animation-lab.ts` owns motion comparison using two instances of the same WebGPU pipeline, independent perspective cameras, native renderers and temporal histories, displayed side by side with linked orbit controls.
- `electron/main.cjs` serves the built renderer through the secure local lantern://app scheme with sandboxing, context isolation, and no Node integration. It supplies the initial display refresh rate for the default FPS cap.

## Area replacement

`src/levels/` owns typed area definitions, validation, seeded decoration and shared runtime construction. See [level design](LEVEL_DESIGN.md). Named area-change requests distinguish travel from appearance refresh; both prepare resources before commit. Area construction uses one guarded cleanup path across initial allocation, asynchronous loading and final batching; pending texture callbacks settle before failed candidates release their resources. The coordinator's area candidate owns geometry, movement and prepared lighting until eligibility and precommit callbacks succeed, releasing rejected, superseded or throwing candidates exactly once. Area replacement releases its effects/instances and keeps renderer/shared asset caches; gameplay owns numeric boundaries, spawn/reset and gate state. Development-only authoring consumes this same runtime through a narrow automation bridge.

## Simulation and pause

Simulation operations mutate an explicitly owned encounter: create, reset, attack, and step. Updates return ordered animation, hit, label and outcome events. Presentation synchronizes transforms, then consumes events; rendering never decides health or victory. Player contacts resolve before movement and enemy attacks. Player input, movement and cooldowns advance once per frame; each authored enemy then advances its own AI. Clip durations remain inputs rather than render-owned clocks inside simulation.

Pausing, inspection, equipment loading and graph preparation stop simulation. Hidden/minimized gameplay clears held input and suspends simulation/rendering without catch-up time; visible unfocused play retains its existing behavior. Frozen views and menus draw 64 settling frames, then sleep until input, camera, settings, resize, scene changes or an explicit authoring frame request. HTML animation remains independent. Hidden Electron inspection opts in through `inspection=render`. The coordinator combines menu, inspection and preparation gates. Developer Restart resets encounter state, session chests/drops/portals, resource depletion, animation actions, effects and temporal history together. Adventure and Harvesting reset their session state while collected items, claims, skills, shelter, stash and Rested remain character progress. Camera follow preserves a fixed 35-degree downward pitch and 45-degree azimuth, with default zoom 1.35, and eases only its focus point. This is an ARPG-style approximation, not a verified camera specification from another game. Temporal sampling offsets are render-only; exactly one temporal resolve owns jitter, and the unjittered camera is restored before the next gameplay frame. Avoid moving runtime rules into a renderer or Options callback.

## Reconstruction and presentation

FSR keeps presentation at fixed pixel ratio 1 and reduces only scene buffers according to Resolution Quality. The beauty pass omits unused normals; the reactive mask uses a single-channel half-float target and full-screen composites omit depth attachments. AO writes only view normals and depth in an independent opaque geometry pass and the native material `getAO` context hook, occluding indirect diffuse/specular light while preserving direct lighting and emission. The opaque FSR comparison uses that same AO context. AO precedes reconstruction. FSR sharpening is part of reconstruction; restrained DOF follows the resolved output with a jitter-corrected bilateral depth guide, followed by bloom, grading, ACES and one sRGB conversion. FSR uses a matched opaque-only pass and raw-color difference reactive mask; no scene visibility or materials are temporarily changed. Startup FSR failure produces an actionable error; runtime errors after successful rendering remain visible errors. Simulation remains independent of resolution quality.

## Pointer aim

Pointer coordinates stay in input; the coordinator reprojects them every active frame using the last displayed unjittered camera and the player's ground-height plane. Simulation receives an optional numeric aim point, applies facing after movement, and ignores points within 0.15 m. Pointer facing takes precedence over movement facing while free; attacks commit facing at acceptance, hit locks hold it, and dodges commit WASD direction or stationary pointer aim. Clearing input also clears aim, so menus and transitions require a fresh pointer event. Cleared or suppressed keyboard holds require a fresh press; autorepeat cannot restore them. Compatible forward/backward/strafe loops follow actual collision-resolved displacement relative to aim. Normalized gait phases align foot contacts; blocking uses directional walking legs beneath its shield pose.

## Camera follow

Camera follow owns a ground-plane anchor with a 0.15 m radial dead zone and a separately eased height. Actual collision-resolved displacement produces 0.20 seconds of look-ahead, capped at 0.65 m; anchor and lead ease exponentially at 8 per second. Lead is suppressed inside the dead zone. Pointer turning never moves the camera. Restart, area arrival and inspection return reset follow state while retaining the player's chosen zoom; pauses and fixed authoring views discard velocity samples without moving the view. The fixed offset remains owned by `src/clearing/projection.ts`.

## Navigation ownership

`src/gameplay/movement.ts` owns navcat generation/path queries and Rapier grounded collision/line-of-sight behind a numeric simulation interface. Area data supplies explicit proxies and optional slope triangles plus stable harvestable trunk proxies, independent of asset availability. Tree-state changes commit collision immediately; one microtask builds and submits their final navigation geometry to the worker, coalescing session depletion applied during arrival. Stale worker revisions remain ineligible. The coordinator replaces/disposes this adapter with the area and passes desired movement through it before presenting transforms.

## Portal presentation

`src/rendering/portal.ts` owns the generic gold/amber portal and bounded decal; the retained analytic swirl uses TSL through the existing graph. Portal effects pause with gameplay; portal destinations and lifetime belong to Adventure. See [library trials](LIBRARY_TRIALS.md).

## Travel and fire safety

Homestead is a safe area: movement remains active, enemies and combat outcomes are absent, and safe healing fires restore 3% maximum health per gameplay second within 3 m. Fires are blocked by a living enemy within 10 m or a pursuing/returning enemy anywhere in the area; Adventure applies the same rule to source interaction, travel commit and inactive destinations. Campfire menus pause simulation; a two-second Scroll of Return cast runs alongside combat and movement, with death taking precedence. Travel commits only after destination geometry/navigation loads, retaining player health and inactive enemy health/defeat plus ground drops. Entry clears transient attack actions rather than replaying attacks across scenes. Return departures retain ground height for portal placement, interaction and arrival. A portal pair survives campfire travel and expires after returning through its home end, death or application restart. Death offers Return Home with collected scrolls retained.

## Save recovery

Local character storage (`lantern.character.v1`) uses revision 7: individual item IDs, quantities, bag coordinates, two equipped main/off sets, eight shared armor/accessory slots, six action assignments, potions, discovered fires, Woodcutting/Mining/Axe Combat XP, collected chest weapon claims, shelter restoration, stash contents, remaining Rested time, gold and ten-entry buyback. The 12 × 8 bag holds variable-size gear and multiple 99-unit supply stacks; equipped items use no bag cells. Revisions 1–6 migrate without discarding progress, including saved recovery entries for legacy quantities beyond capacity. Scroll/Wood totals and the type-based renderer loadout are derived, never separately saved. `gameplay/character-save.ts` decodes and migrates unpublished candidates; `character-persistence.ts` owns silent storage recovery. The primary storage key remains unchanged. Before replacing a valid primary, its bytes become `lantern.character.v1.backup`; an unreadable primary restores a validated backup automatically. Unreadable primary/backup bytes are deduplicated into JSON string arrays at each key's `.unreadable` suffix before replacement; failed preservation leaves the existing copies untouched. Recovery adds no player messages or decisions.

Persistence keeps one latest unsaved snapshot and retries failed operations after 1, 2, 5, 15 and then 30 seconds, capped at 30 seconds. New changes coalesce during backoff; success resets it, and page exit attempts a final synchronous flush and cancels retries. Startup retries an unavailable storage read twice (after 1 and 2 seconds), then permits in-memory play. Later access can save safely if the store proves empty or contains only preserved unreadable data. A late read of valid existing progress blocks replacement for that session and retains the active in-memory character; the next startup loads that progress. Permanently unavailable storage cannot guarantee durability. Recovery diagnostics are exposed only through development authoring. Keybinding changes also apply immediately and retry failed writes silently; their menu closes after a valid change even when storage is unavailable.

## Loot ownership

Adventure owns ground-drop identities, quantity, reachable landing position/height, 0.55-second landing clock and pickup target. Supplies collect within 1.5 m after landing only when a nearby physical route exists; equipment requires an explicit label/object click and never auto-equips. Pickup navigation has an independent path query, leaving enemy pursuit caches intact. Travel/death retain drops in area sessions; restart refreshes them. Dropped supplies wait for departure/re-entry before automatic recollection. The clearing chest scatters two scrolls and two Health Potions once per session and offers each unclaimed weapon; collection records permanent claims. Harvesting contacts create Wood, Stone or Iron drops with XP provenance; only successfully collected quantities award gathering XP.

## Inventory commits

Menus pause gameplay. Inventory rearrangement, splitting, packing and equipment displacement compute candidates first; asset/motion preparation completes before equipped-state changes commit. Dragging outside drops items onto reachable local ground. A Scroll of Return cast reserves one scroll against dropping until completion or death. Each enemy has a 50% one-scroll reward, 200 health and 20 direct damage. Authored weapon Basics deal 45–70 damage before equipment bonuses; starter Skills scale weapon damage by 120%. Armor reduces all direct hits independently of shield blocking. Values and resource-cap rules belong to [combat and equipment](EQUIPMENT.md); broader economy and enemy renewal remain later work.

## Rendering contract

Optional outlines are owned by `src/rendering/outlines.ts` within the shared graph. Mesh instances carry actor/prop strength; unregistered terrain, vegetation and effects default to zero. The environment surface family mapping selects solid scenery, and batching keys retain that classification. When enabled, an extra scene MRT attachment records strength with the beauty pass's cutouts, deformation and jitter. Soft inward mask/depth contours darken scene color before FSR, using the same depth and velocity without another geometry pass or temporal filter. FSR's raw opaque/translucent comparison stays unchanged. Off omits the active outline attachment and contour processing; the existing bounded graph cache may retain a recently used appearance. Toggle commits reset temporal history. Outlines default/reset to On and persist independently of other graphics choices.

All routes require native WebGPU. There is one visual pipeline in `src/rendering/webgpu-pipeline.ts`; gameplay, authoring and animation comparison use it. FSR Temporal is the sole reconstruction method, with Balanced and sharpening 0.50 as fresh/reset defaults. Output pixel ratio is always 1. SMAA/FXAA and the WebGL composer are removed. Unsupported native initialization stops startup with an actionable error; there is no backend substitution. The renderer's r186 `_getFallback` integration and backend compilation-error bridge must be revalidated on three.js upgrades. The bridge rejects shader failures that three.js otherwise logs while resolving `compileAsync`. Depth conversion supports orthographic and perspective cameras.

Legacy renderer URL parameters are ignored; retired rendering-method, render-scale and volumetric fields are ignored. Settings revision 6 retains the migration from old High/Low quality to independent shadow and particle presets, preserving applicable player preferences. CLI renderer overrides accept only the compatibility value `webgpu`. `scripts/check-rendering.mjs`, included in the handoff gate, prevents application WebGL support, legacy shader hooks, direct scene rendering and additional render-graph owners. Third-party packages may still contain unused backend code.

`src/rendering/surface-detail.ts` owns material-only 16× filtering, conservative resolution-derived mip bias and bounded POM sampling. Pipeline contexts supply per-graph bias and the compiled Texture Depth choice; cached On/Off graphs have independent uniforms/history. Geometry AO and irradiance preparation omit parallax; beauty and opaque FSR comparison share it. Material coordinates use physical surface gradients for orthographic/perspective cameras and preserve geometric depth/velocity/shadows. Loaded standard materials retain authored maps; prepared environment materials read height/cavity/parallax-mask fields from version-3 packed channels (height/roughness/metalness/eligibility in metallic-roughness RGBA, cavity in normal alpha) or legacy version-1/2 sidecars, recorded in lighting fingerprints and build dependencies. `woodland-ground.ts` owns coherent ground projections and normal/height-aware blending. Recipe and private-bake ownership stays in the environment manifest/exporter.

## Asset and resource ownership

The local [character gallery](CHARACTERS.md) owns roster comparison and per-rig sample previews under `src/labs/characters/`; private conversion and captures live in `scripts/assets/characters/`. It uses the shared WebGPU pipeline and never changes the playable actor.

Original texture studies are tracked; downloaded sources and vendor exports are ignored. Exporters read private sources under `.local/` and write only Lantern outputs. Mixamo is the sole animation provider. Library instances release their owned skeletons; the library owns cached shared textures, geometry and prepared node materials. Shutdown waits for accepted nested loads and releases shared art once. Assembly construction waits for sibling loads before releasing failed partial rigs; failed material variants release unpublished model instances without disposing borrowed art. Cached library GLTFs retain only their scene and assembly bindposes, not parsers. Failed library requests leave their caches so an explicit equipment or area retry can load again. `assets/resource-ownership.ts` tracks shared bitmap lifetimes. `assets/scene-cache.ts` leases active/loading prepared scenes and retains at most 256 MiB of unused scenes for repeat travel; repeated URL instances share unchanged material/texture semantics. `assets/scenery-loader.ts` uses the pinned KTX2 decoder and retires its worker pool after completed loads, recreating workers only for later uncached art. Renderer/effects owners retain their cleanup responsibilities.

Production builds stage explicitly selected Synty library entries plus dependency closure, authored playable models and complete per-rig motion catalogs, referenced area/environment art and file dependencies. Prepared lighting copies only indexed outputs. Development lab routes and gallery assets are excluded. This is a local-build convenience, not publication clearance. See [development](DEVELOPMENT.md) and [third-party notices](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## Change method

Read the affected owner and consumers first. Preserve existing work; introduce dependencies or abstractions only for concrete consumers. Update this guide when ownership changes. Keep decisions beside their owner rather than only in an implementation plan. Extend the cheap simulation tests only for established behavior that merits ongoing protection.

## HUD and player controls

Health and mana use original text-prompted ImageGen frames under `assets/ui/orbs/`, with clipped animated red/blue liquid and smooth fill/drain transitions owned by `src/ui/orbs.css`. Gameplay owns health maxima and the shared 100-point mana resource. Enemy health appears above the enemy for three gameplay seconds after damage; projection uses the unjittered camera. Death, reset and travel clear its visibility.

Left click activates its assigned action-bar ability, with loot and world interactions taking priority. Shift starts a 2.4 m dodge over 0.45 seconds with 0.25 seconds of invulnerability and a one-second cooldown. It uses the same collision and area boundary rules as walking, including safe areas and cleared encounters. Attack/hit locks block dodge; dodge blocks attack. Player-only Mixamo Dive Roll From Standing is trimmed from 1.25–3.55 source seconds and scaled to the dodge clock. The five base roles remain, with directional, block, chop, mine, Skill and dodge roles supplied by compatible weapon profiles. Whole-canvas invulnerability flashing is removed; damage feedback stays local. Travel/reset clears transient dodge state. Hit sparks remain; weapon trails are removed.

B toggles Inventory; Escape closes the open menu or opens Options. Menus pause play, clear movement input, and dismiss on a click that begins and ends outside their panel, consuming that click. Fire shadows remain enabled; gameplay has no camera-following blanket fill. Settings revision 6 preserves applicable saved choices, including earlier sharpening values, while retaining the quality-preset migration. Fresh/reset sharpening is 0.50. Later player choices persist; comparison URLs remain temporary. DOF is a saved Off/Soft/Cinematic mode, with Cinematic as the fresh/reset default and old numeric values ignored. Fixed profiles belong to the shared WebGPU pipeline, which tracks camera-target focus and bypasses DOF when Off. Changing Soft/Cinematic updates uniforms; crossing Off rebuilds the graph. The character gallery explicitly disables DOF.

## Solo caster encounter

Forest Clearing contains the camp raider and a separate caster off the woodland approach. `layout.enemy` and optional `layout.caster` author their spawns; `Encounter.enemies` owns each actor's health, clocks, home, engagement and return state. Their notice areas are separated. Each enemy has its own collider, navigation route, compatible actor presentation, damage health bar and once-per-session scroll reward. Adventure preserves both states on travel/death; permanent character saves are unchanged. The camp chest names its guard and unlocks when that raider dies, independent of the caster. Fire safety evaluates every living enemy under the existing proximity/pursuit rules. The query-selected replacement encounter has been removed.

Encounter owns each enemy archetype and actor-specific projectile `owner`. The caster approaches within 6 m and visible ground, commits facing at cast acceptance, remains planted through windup/recovery, and releases one straight 8 m/s bolt at its compatible clip marker. Prepared Goblin casting is 1.6 seconds with release at 0.8 seconds; cooldown adds 0.5 seconds after the action. Hits interrupt windup through the existing reaction rule. Bolts use swept terrain/actor collision, consume on dodge contact, and use incoming direction for frontal shield damage reduction. Defeating an enemy clears only that enemy's bolts. A local victory leaves the distant fight available; the area is fully won only when all placed enemies are defeated. Pause freezes all cast/projectile clocks; travel clears transient actions and preserves enemy health/defeat.

`rendering/projectiles.ts` presents a cool pale-blue enemy bolt, compact charge and short release flash through node materials in the shared graph. Gameplay never reads visual lifetime. The caster uses its own staff profile while the camp guard retains the axe profile. Animation catalog loading uses rig identity separately from actor identity. Staff profiles explicitly name a compatible grip clip, baked to the actor's own rig.

## Equipment and trees

`gameplay/equipment.ts` owns item identity, hand occupancy and authored grips; `rendering/equipment.ts` stages releasable Synty instances on verified rig sockets. The coordinator prepares the model and compatible motions before committing, without resetting enemies or rewards; motion experimentation remains in the development animation lab. ## Player projectiles

Encounter owns timed 12 m player arrows/bolts and swept damage/terrain obstruction; `rendering/projectiles.ts` only presents those records.

## Input buffering

Input buffering holds one attack, ability, dodge or weapon-swap request for 150 ms and clears with menus, travel, death and reset. A buffered attack starts at its recovery unlock; only the remaining portion of that frame advances its contacts and new cooldown. Safe and cleared areas retain basic attacks.

## Gathering ownership

`levels/trees.ts` identifies standing trees before surface substitution; `levels/resources.ts` extends those stable IDs with authored mineral nodes and resource-level data. `gameplay/harvesting.ts` owns depletion, facing selection and the 120-second active-gameplay renewal clock, deferring occupied locations. It skips renewal scans until the earliest pending deadline; registration recomputes that deadline and occupied due nodes retry on the next active frame. The coordinator routes loot clicks before contextual gathering, checks nearby/pursuing threats, owns approach cancellation and synchronizes verified contact clocks. `rendering/gathering-tools.ts` temporarily hides combat attachments without changing the saved loadout. Builder preserves resource roots through batching; tree collision changes rebuild routes while depleted mineral cores remain solid. Standing geometry prepares lighting before depletion is reapplied, so gathering never triggers bakes.

## Gathering progress and shelter

`gameplay/skills.ts` owns derived gathering levels, yield/XP formulas, Rested tuning and the authored shelter recipe. Adventure awards harvested XP only for collected quantities through drop provenance; transferred or re-dropped materials cannot grant more. It also owns repair consumption, lossless paired inventory/stash transfers (including a 99-unit cap when dragging overflow into an empty cell) and active Rested time. `rendering/shelter.ts` presents the open timber-and-canvas cover; the prepared area appearance and repair consumption commit together. Inventory presents skill progress and Rested without rebuilding grids on each timer tick. See [gathering and shelter](GATHERING.md).

## Area lighting

Every route uses the shared Golden preset from `levels/lighting-preset.ts`; area definitions author only small overrides. Alternate presets, random moods, lighting URL switches and legacy full lighting definitions are removed. See [lighting authoring](LIGHTING.md). The resolver supplies `environment`, `probes`, `saturation`, `grade` and preset distance fog, deriving initial probe coverage from the playable boundary. Environment skies are original floating-point radiance independent of the backdrop, prefiltered once with native WebGPU PMREM. Static scenery is cloned into an isolated bake scene without actors, portals, particles or local animated lights. The native `LightProbeGrid` addon captures surface radiance from the sun and environment, adding local diffuse bounce without baking readability fill or hemisphere energy. This preparation uses the addon internally; gameplay and labs still use the single shared visual graph.

Preparation is serialized and suspends presentation while the renderer is capturing probes. Probe signatures include only resolved bake lighting, loaded geometry/instances/materials, source bytes, procedural surfaces and surface mode. Gameplay metadata does not invalidate GI. Commit attaches the prepared grid/environment only after area construction and travel checks succeed. Use renderer-wide LRU limits of eight skies / 32 MiB and eight probe appearances / 16 MiB; committed and candidate leases prevent premature disposal. Cancelled candidates release their leases. Prepared native atlases load by fingerprint, with live baking retained when prepared data is unavailable. Dispose cached resources with Graphics. Authoring changes rebake, unchanged revisits reuse the cache. Native PCF supplies sun and point-light shadows; grass receives scenery shadows without casting blades. Main sun shadows fit visible ground/raised scenery, snap their center to shadow texels, and use a bounded depth envelope including offscreen static casters. Shadowed fires use their point light directly; authored intensity survives graphics updates, with a small warmth multiplier and flicker.

The verified Paladin/Goblin legacy FBX material conversion clears imported untextured metalness: Blender maps Phong ReflectionFactor to Metallic, which incorrectly makes body skin/cloth conductive. Authored color, normal and specular textures survive. PBR GLB sources bypass this correction; the playable Paladin retains its original authored textures.

Environment palette, material treatment, the fixed Golden woodland baseline and local surface preparation follow [the art direction](ART_DIRECTION.md).

`player-lantern.ts` owns the optional personal lantern prototype, its privately loaded cage model, warm primary point light and short-range owner bounce. The coordinator stages area/surface changes, commits successful changes and toggles the personal light without rebuilding the area. The light attaches to the verified player rig and stays outside static scenery/probe preparation. Renderer disposal on page exit releases the native device along with lighting/effect resources.

Shadow and particle presets are owned by `src/rendering/quality-presets.ts`. Shadow map changes release old targets; quality never changes authored light count, coverage, bias or softness. Particle presets affect active capacity, continuous emission and weather rate, retaining combat burst timing/counts. Volumetric rendering and bounded mist regions are retired.

## Effect resource lifetime

`CoreEffects` tracks live particle slots separately from each preset's capacity. Empty pools stay invisible and skip simulation/buffer uploads; spawning reactivates them, while expiration, clearing and quality reductions retire slots. Live particle formulas and emission order remain unchanged. Water and foam share their identical wave-position attribute and release their geometries together. Actor locomotion retains its own displacement/weight scratch storage rather than allocating it each frame.

## Presentation updates

The action bar keeps references to its fixed elements and mutates text, attributes and disabled states only when their displayed values change. Inventory and repair menus retain the latest character reference while closed and refresh on opening; bag/stash comparison and Rested presentation run only while their menu is open. Audio reads only position coordinates and iterates its owned voice set directly; its scheduling, voice limits and oldest-voice eviction remain unchanged.

## Graphics preparation

Graphics settings arrive as immutable snapshots. Ordinary values update once per presentation frame without resizing or clearing temporal history. Structural changes settle for 150 ms; closing Options flushes the latest request. The shared pipeline serializes candidate preparation, holds the last image while compiling, commits only the newest successful request and retains the working graph on failure. Resolution Quality resizes existing scene buffers; two recently used effect graphs are retained at most. Retired graphs release r186 render bindings for their own pass targets as well as node resources. Options remains HTML on the browser main thread; worker isolation is not implemented.

## Action bar and input ownership

`src/clearing/encounter-presentation.ts` owns actor transforms, animation events, hit bursts and actor presentation resets. It forwards the same ordered events to gameplay audio and the HUD; the clearing coordinator retains weapon-set progress, XP and input interruption decisions.

`src/gameplay/abilities.ts` owns stable ability IDs, weapon requirements, tap/hold behavior, costs and cooldowns. Encounter accepts all six slots through `useAbility`, routes to the current compatible weapon set or its alternate, and emits a weapon-set event before presentation. Accepted actions snapshot their contact timings, weapon and damage; piercing projectiles retain their own once-per-enemy contacts. Cooldowns are keyed by ability, so duplicate assignments and weapon changes cannot refresh them. Mana regenerates at 8 per active gameplay second. Travel retains mana, Skill and potion cooldowns; menus freeze them.

Inventory represents both sets using the existing main/off placements and a `weaponSet` index (absent legacy values mean set I). Adventure's revision 5 save retains `activeSet`, six `actionBar` ability IDs, potion stacks, and all revision 1–4 progress/recovery entries. Migration grants three potions once, using overflow when needed. Invalid bytes follow the preservation and backup recovery rules above before any replacement. Input preferences live independently under `lantern.bindings.v1` and receive injected storage from the clearing boot seam.

`src/input/bindings.ts` is the stable entry point for the binding model, keyboard normalization and preferences. `src/input/binding-model.ts` owns the single ordered action table used to derive names, defaults, menu groups and binding eligibility; `src/input/keyboard.ts` owns physical-code normalization; `src/input/preferences.ts` owns injected preference storage and retry scheduling. The entry point exposes named input actions, the ordered six slot inputs shared with the action bar/Keybindings, and physical bindings; clearing input reports actions and held states without deciding combat. `src/ui/combat.ts` owns the live action bar and Skills editor. The editor moves the shared bar into its native modal layer; editing is unavailable during combat/actions. `src/ui/keybindings.ts` owns a draft, conflict resolution and Apply/Cancel. UI selection and click-to-interact consume events before gameplay slot activation.

The inventory controller stages both weapon sets and their compatible motions before changing equipped items. Retained equipment instances allow synchronous swaps without fetching or pausing combat. Gathering uses separately prepared basic axes/pickaxes and compatible Chop/Mine motions; neither ownership nor combat equipment changes. Player damage, other actions and menus interrupt the approach/harvest.

Area construction retains selectable tree, chest and fire roots through batching. Clicks approach free interaction points through the numeric movement adapter, recheck availability/reach, and invoke the existing Adventure operations. `src/rendering/interaction-highlight.ts` presents an expanded back-face brass contour in the shared scene graph with depth occlusion, independently of cosmetic Outlines.

## Prototype conventions

- Keep gameplay and render state explicit in TypeScript. The camera is fixed isometric with follow and scroll zoom; WASD or arrows move, six assignable action slots default to Q/E/R/G/LMB/RMB, Shift dodges, F uses a Health Potion, T uses a Scroll of Return, Tab swaps weapon sets, K opens Skills, B toggles Inventory, and Escape toggles Options or closes a menu. Click world objects to approach and interact; all gameplay controls are remappable through Keybindings.
- Gathering never requires owned or equipped tools. Show presentation-only basic tools with compatible Mixamo gathering motions, and restore combat presentation without changing inventory or weapon sets.
- Keep the encounter runnable when optional scenery is absent; report missing playable character art clearly.
- Select animation clips only from a verified compatible rig. Gameplay uses Paladin J Nordstrom for the player and Goblin D Shareyko for the enemy, with curated Mixamo clips baked independently to each rig. The playable Paladin uses its original authored textures; retired palette experiments remain privately archived. Original text-prompted surfaces are projected and baked locally. Use Mixamo exclusively for both the clearing and the comparison lab. Do not reintroduce the discarded animation providers. Preserve the full source catalog privately, and lazy-load only selected GLB clips.
- After gameplay edits, use one short browser interaction flow demonstrating the changed behavior. Choose the relevant portion of the optional smoke references; do not replay the entire game checklist.


## Gold, trading and merchant ownership

Adventure owns wallet collection and atomic buy/sell/buyback commits; `gameplay/economy.ts` owns the formula and fixed catalog. `ui/shop.ts` presents the trade menu, reusing `ui/equipment-details.ts` with Inventory for equipment comparisons. Ground items extend inventory items with gold without making it a bag item. Revision 7 stores the wallet and ten-entry buyback alongside existing progress; all container/buyback IDs remain unique. [Loot](LOOT.md#gold-and-homestead-trading) owns the rules and values.

Area `shop` data names its counter, interaction point and privately prepared merchant model/transform. Builder retains both counter and merchant targets through batching. `rendering/merchant.ts` owns the NPC's verified packed idle, mixer and complete resource disposal; it remains outside static irradiance preparation and follows the paused area clock. The shared native pipeline and Golden/local-lantern recipes remain authoritative.
